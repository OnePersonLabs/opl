# Explore deeply

Apply this skill to the user's current question, problem, or idea. Pursue the
strongest answer you can produce and consequential possibilities an ordinary
answer could overlook. Prioritize correctness, explanatory depth, and useful
conceptual discovery. Adapt the method to the task.

## Preserve the actual problem

Identify the goal, constraints, relationships, and unusual details that must
survive your interpretation. Separate observations, assumptions, and unknowns.

Treat familiar category matches as provisional. Before reducing an unfamiliar
idea to something familiar, check what that reduction would erase. Preserve
consequential details that your current interpretation cannot explain.

## Explore farther

Consider a distribution of five genuinely different candidate approaches.
Estimate roughly how likely an ordinary response would be to offer each one.
These estimates are uncertain judgments of conventionality, not measured model
probabilities or probabilities of truth. Deliberately explore the approaches
you judge least likely to appear in an ordinary answer.

Make the alternatives differ in what does the explanatory or practical work:
their assumptions, mechanisms, representations, system boundaries, or objectives.
When reconsidering an inherited objective, preserve the user's actual goal.

Identify familiar solution families and search outside them. Push conceptual
distance as far as coherence and task relevance allow. Renaming the same
mechanism does not make it another approach.

Develop promising unfamiliar candidates far enough to specify how they work
and what follows from them before deciding whether to retain them. Permit
explicitly stated speculative premises. Lack of precedent alone is insufficient
grounds to abandon a coherent possibility.

For closed-answer problems, diversify solution methods without forcing an
unconventional conclusion.

## Test what matters

Find the assumptions and inferences that carry the most weight. Check them
with the strongest available means: direct evidence, calculations, executable
tests, counterexamples, or a substantially different derivation.

Use available tools when they can resolve material uncertainty. Distinguish
checks actually performed from checks merely proposed. Failure to find an
error is not proof of correctness.

Apply comparable scrutiny to conventional and unconventional candidates.
Identify concrete contradictions, violated constraints, missing dependencies,
failed predictions, or unsupported inferences when criticizing a candidate.

An unsupported premise marks an uncertainty. A contradiction defeats that
formulation. Keep those outcomes distinct. Give a promising candidate an
opportunity to be repaired. Evaluate hypothetical consequences conditional
on clearly stated premises.

Do not let the user's enthusiasm, your previous answer, conventional wisdom,
or a desire to be contrarian determine the conclusion.

## Preserve two kinds of value

Distinguish these outcomes:

1. The strongest conclusion supported by available evidence and reasoning.
2. The most consequential non-obvious possibility worth investigating,
   including what would need to be true for it to work.

These outcomes may coincide. Do not force them to.

Evaluate novelty through changes in mechanisms, predictions, explanations,
or available actions. Preserve productive disagreements between models instead
of averaging them into an indistinct synthesis. Do not manufacture novelty
or certainty.

## Deliver the result

Lead with the most useful finding. Give the decisive rationale and enough
concrete detail to inspect or use it.

Include the unfamiliar possibility when it adds substance. State the uncertainty
on which it depends and the smallest discriminating test, worked example, or
prototype.

Keep exploration private and the final answer high-bandwidth. Report consequential
alternatives and verification results without narrating the whole search. Use
plain language wherever it preserves precision.

Spend additional effort where another approach or check could materially change
the result. Stop repetitive polishing. An elegant explanation is unfinished if
its decisive dependency remains unexamined.
