# Validate perceptual comprehension

Use this when selecting variants, defining acceptance criteria, or reviewing an implementation.

1. **Choose discriminating scenes.** Include minimal pairs that differ in a crucial semantic distinction, not just pretty or common states. Include degraded inputs, conflict, stale data, stable success, and transitions that can invalidate advice.
2. **Show at realistic apparent size and exposure.** Match distance, display size, luminance, context, and competing attention. Pick exposure time from the task and treat it as a test target, not a universal rule.
3. **Ask behavior first.** After hiding the image ask, “What is happening? What would you do next? What supports that? How certain are you?” Avoid teaching a legend just before the test unless training is truly part of deployment.
4. **Probe alternate conditions.** Repeat selected scenes as still frames, without color, at low resolution, under expected lighting, with interrupted viewing, and with reduced motion. For spatial displays, check whether participants identify the coordinate frame and compare the correct variables.
5. **Replay time-dependent cases.** Feed variants identical sequences; check whether guidance thrashes, persists past invalidation, or implies fabricated intermediate values. Test that one missed transition does not make the present frame uninterpretable.
6. **Record error and recovery.** Track wrong or delayed action, false confidence, inability to tell missing evidence from success, unnecessary adjustment, number of repeat glances, and time to appropriate action. Include participant explanation to identify which encoding failed.
7. **Revise and retest.** Prioritize consequential confusions; change the semantic channel or state contract when necessary. Preference ratings may supplement but cannot substitute for comprehension.

State assumptions and sample limitations. Do not claim that a contrast ratio, a brief internal review, or a few successful screenshots prove field legibility or user comprehension.
